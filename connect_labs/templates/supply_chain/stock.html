How this works

Two ways to record stock

  • Stock in or out: something moved (a delivery, a handout, a loss).
  • Count: what is actually on the shelf. A count is compared with the records and shows any difference. It replaces the records only if you mark it as an override.

Which number counts? Supply's records are the official figure.

More on records and self-reports

This holds for a field worker as much as for a store. A field worker's own balance on their phone is shown as a self-report, beside the records, with the difference. These forms are the fallback: regular handovers need the simpler route.

How cover is measured

Cover is days of stock: what is on hand, at the rate this point has been using it (from {{ min_rate_days }} days of use on). It is measured against each point's own minimum and maximum, so "below its band" means below what this programme set. Goods in transit are excluded: they are real, but they are not cover. Each point is topped up to its maximum; below its minimum it is overdue. A point with none above it in the network is restocked by reordering from its supplier.

Where demand comes from

Demand is read from the consumption movements on each store's ledger, whoever recorded them — a Connect visit, a partner's report or a clerk's entry. A store that dispenses nothing itself, such as a distributor's warehouse, is rated on what it releases to other supply points. A worker's reported figure is kept beside the ledger rather than overwriting it; moving the ledger takes a deliberate override with a reason.
