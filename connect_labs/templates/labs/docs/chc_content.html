System Design (CCHQ App)

Section 1

Program context

Connect-CHC delivers a digitally managed Learn–Deliver–Verify–Pay (LDVP) model for essential child health interventions using Dimagi's CommCare platform through CommCare Connect. Frontline Workers (FLWs) visit households and deliver four services to children under five: MUAC malnutrition screening, ORS & Zinc distribution, deworming (Albendazole), and vaccine counseling. The system embeds eligibility logic, data validation, GPS stamping, and photo verification to ensure service fidelity and reduce fraud, with offline-first data capture on low-cost Android devices and automated incentive calculation tied to verified outputs.

Key terms

Household (HH)A family unit visited by the FLW within a Work Area, where under-5 (U5) children may be registered for service delivery.
Service Delivery (SD)Delivering a health service to an eligible child and recording it in the app. Appears as the "CHC Service Delivery" Deliver Unit type — payment accrues per child visit.
Work Area (WA)A ~50m × 50m geographic square assigned to an FLW. The FLW must be physically inside an allocated WA to deliver services.
Work Area Group (WAG)A bundle of WAs shown to the FLW one group at a time. A WAG must be fully completed before the next group is unlocked.
Deliver Unit (DU)The unit against which payment accrues per verified visit. Two types: CHC Service Delivery (one paid unit per child served) and No Children Found WA (pays the equivalent of one child visit).
Payment Unit (PU)A grouping against which the FLW's delivery progress and payment are tracked on Connect.

Legacy note (2025 design)

The 2025 app used deliver-unit cases with manual check-in / check-out forms and a "Change service area" flow. The 2026 redesign replaced these with work-area cases and auto-checkin — the form itself resolves which WA the FLW is standing in, so no separate check-in step exists.

Registration & delivery are deliberately split (chosen design: "Option 4")

One Register HH & Children form creates the household and all child cases via a repeat group; a separate, simple Child Service Delivery form (case type child) records each delivery. One submission of the delivery form = one Deliver Unit for that child, which Connect handles cleanly, while the FLW's mental model stays "go to the household, do everything from there."

The three-way inaccessible distinction — always read both properties

hq_status_wa alone cannot tell a pending, approved, and rejected request apart — it stays inaccessible_requested in all three. Any form condition that blocks on hq_status_wa = 'inaccessible_requested' alone will wrongly block rejected WAs.

EXPECTED_VISIT_REACHED is informational on Connect only — it does not block the FLW: registration and delivery remain allowed after the expected visit count is reached.

Payment eligibility logic (Service Delivery Checks)

A calculations block in the Health Service Delivery form decides whether a visit qualifies for payment. Each of four checks returns 1 (pass) or 0 (fail), where pass means the service was delivered OR the child was legitimately ineligible:

CheckPasses when…
mal_check — MUACMeasurement taken AND photo captured; or parent refused MUAC consent; or child < 6 months (ineligible by age).
ors_check — ORS + ZincORS photo captured AND handover confirmed. No age exemption — ORS applies to all U5 children.
dw_check — DewormingDeworming delivered; or child received a dose within the last 6 months; or child is unwell today; or child < 12 months.
vc_check — Vaccine CounselingCounseling completed (vaccine questionnaire answered AND completion confirmed). Binary — no exemptions.

The master flag: all_service_del_checks = 1 only when all four checks pass, else 0. A display flag converts it to a Yes/No the FLW sees on the summary screen.

Deduplication strategy

Duplicates are handled at two levels — in the app (blocking) and on the Connect platform (flagging):

  • Household / family uniqueness (in-app, blocking): each set of parents is a family unit, and the combination of the father's name + mother's name is the family case name. The Household form checks the household casedb — if that name combination already exists among the user's registered households, the app blocks the FLW from registering the household. First and last names are captured as separate required fields on one screen.
  • Child uniqueness: a three-layer check — one blocking check inside the Household form, plus two hidden flags evaluated in the Health Service Delivery (HSD) form that Connect verification rules use to reject suspicious visits. Detailed below.
  • Cross-user duplicates: the app cannot check across users — different users registering the same household or child is possible. That's what the opp-level duplicate verification flags are for.

Every check above runs against the casedb, which has user-level ownership: only households and children registered by the current user are visible to it. Example: FLW1 cannot register two children named Abhishek with DOB 02-04-2020 anywhere in the opportunity — the second is caught as a duplicate. But FLW1 and FLW2 can each register an Abhishek with DOB 02-04-2020, because neither can see the other's cases. Cross-user duplicates can only be caught at the platform level.

The design doc's original child entity-id approach (concatenating father's name + phone number + mother's name + number of children + child's name) was superseded by the implemented checks above.

A scoring-based "fuzzy" matching approach (government ID, phone, GPS proximity, normalized names, overlapping members with score thresholds for auto-merge vs. human review) was documented but explicitly marked won't do.

Learn App & quiz engine

The assessment quiz is powered by a single lookup table, post_assess, holding the full question bank. Each row is one answer option, not a full question — a question spans multiple rows. The app randomises which questions appear:

ColumnUsed byPurpose
question_idLearnUnique ID per question (q1, q2, q3…). Must be continuous with no gaps.
question_textBothThe question shown; identical across all rows of the same question.
answerBothOne option per row; typically 3–4 rows per question.
answer_statusBoth1 = correct, 0 = incorrect. Exactly one row per question is 1. The sum of selected values is the score.
categoryRelearnGroups questions into relearn topics (e.g. vaccine_counseling).
question_indexRelearnSequential number (1…n) within a category; must have no gaps.
question_category—Not used by the app; can be left as is.

Learn randomiser: builds the list of unique question_ids, randomly picks maximum_random_question_count_tobeshown of them (typically 20), renders each with its option rows, and scores by summing answer_status of selected answers.
Relearn randomiser: filters by the selected category, treats question_index 1…n as the bank, and picks randomly within it.

Assessment quiz gating: the quiz unlocks automatically only after all learn modules are completed. It has 20 questions and a pass mark of 80 (16 of 20 correct), with unlimited attempts. On passing and submitting, the FLW receives a push notification on the Connect app that Learn is finished and they can claim the Deliver job. (The relearn quiz is stricter — 100 marks to pass; see Relearning module.)

App propertyModuleMeaning
maximum_number_of_questionLearnHard-coded total bank size (q1…q60 → 60). Must be updated manually whenever questions change.
maximum_random_question_count_tobeshownLearnQuestions shown per quiz attempt (typically 20). Must not exceed bank size.

Why gaps break the quiz

The randomiser builds IDs like q1…qN up to maximum_number_of_question. A missing ID in the middle makes it pick an ID with no rows, producing an empty question slot. After removing questions, always re-number to stay continuous and update the max-count property. The same applies to question_index within each relearn category.

OCS support bot

When the FLW fills the Learn app, the OCS bot is triggered — it appears in the Connect Messaging module. The bot supports FLWs with app navigation and answers common FLW questions (FAQs).

Relearning module

Current flow: Connect task–driven (Master app versions 1673–1904, May–July 2026)

A quality-control mechanism driven entirely from Connect tasks — nothing needs to be done on CommCare HQ. When an FLW needs retraining, a task is triggered on Connect; the Deliver app locks itself to the matching relearn module until the FLW passes it.

  1. Task triggered on Connect: from Connect (audits), a task is triggered for one user or multiple users. The task sets a designated user property to 1. Task types must be configured on Connect before the opportunity launches.
  2. Deliver app locks: form display conditions read the property — all deliver-related forms are hidden and only the relearn-related forms show. Relearn has multiple modules (e.g. deworming, MUAC, service delivery); the module matching the triggered task is the one shown.
  3. FLW completes the module and quiz: the FLW works through the module and attends a quiz with randomised questions drawn from the category of the triggered task. A score of 100 is required to pass.
  4. Unlock: on achieving 100 and submitting the Continue Service Delivery form, the task question is submitted — the task is marked finished on Connect and all relearn-related properties in the Deliver app reset, restoring the deliver forms.
While a task is pending, Connect rejects the FLW's visits

Any service delivery visits submitted while a relearn task is open will be rejected on Connect — the FLW must clear the task before resuming payable work.

Superseded: the App Control / HQ flow

The earlier design — an admin logging into CommCare HQ as the FLW and submitting an App Control form — is no longer used. Everything now runs from Connect.

Step by step

  1. Sync, check the map, travel. The FLW syncs the app at the start, checks the Work Area map, and travels to a WA of their current WAG based on the map.
  2. Arrive — the GPS gate. In the Add Household form, the GPS block captures location (minimum 25m / preferred 15m accuracy). The form then iterates through a loop, running CommCare's inside_polygon() function against each WA of the current WAG. If the FLW is inside any of them, the form says they are inside and service delivery is allowed; if they are inside none, they are not allowed to proceed.
  3. Screen the household. The FLW asks whether U5 children live in the house and whether a parent can be spoken to. If not, they move on — this interaction is not recorded in the app.
  4. Consent. The caregiver/parent consent (participation, sharing basic details, MUAC capture, ORS/Zinc/deworming) is recorded before registration proceeds.
  5. Register household & children. One form registers the household (named by father's + mother's names, with in-app duplicate blocking), the number of children, and every U5 child via a repeat group. Registration is not allowed when the household has no U5 child. Submission creates the household case and child cases and marks the WA visited for HQ — but not yet for Connect. Registering the first household also unhides the Health Service Delivery form.
  6. Deliver services per child. The FLW comes back to the Health Service Delivery form and fills it for each child: deworming (with age/dose/wellness logic; photo optional), MUAC (reading + mandatory photo), ORS/Zinc — 2 co-packs per child for the RCT (mandatory handover photo), vaccine counseling (history + optional card photo). The form checks the FLW's distance from the GPS location captured at household registration: under 50m → delivery allowed; farther → "out of vicinity of the household" and the form cannot proceed. A child marked unwell cannot be revisited for that service — the FLW is still paid for the first visit.
  7. Review the summary and submit. The final screen shows ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ per service and whether the visit qualifies as a complete, valid delivery. As soon as the form is submitted, the delivery is visible on Connect. Each payable child visit is one Deliver Unit.
  8. Edge paths. A WA with no eligible children → No Children Found WA form (the FLW must be inside the WA; pays one visit; only available when nothing has been submitted against that WA). A WA that can't be reached → Inaccessible WA request, which can be filled from anywhere geographically (goes to NM review; see below).
  9. Complete the WAG. When every WA is attended (delivered / no-children / inaccessible), the FLW submits the Change WAG form to get their next WAG — the geographically closest one (see Auto-WAG Selection).

Auto-WAG Selection

Available from: Master app version 1949

Auto-WAG Selection automatically assigns Work Area Groups (WAGs) to FLWs in a geographically logical order, replacing the earlier logic where the next WAG was picked arbitrarily from an unordered list (which could send an FLW north one day, south the next, and back north again).

It has two parts:

First WAG SelectionAssigns the north-west most WAG when a user has no WAG yet.
Next WAG SelectionAssigns the geographically closest WAG when the user finishes their current one.

How it works — for everyone (non-technical explanation)

Think of the FLW's territory as a map with several Work Area Groups (WAGs) they need to cover, one at a time — each WAG being a bundle of Work Areas (WAs).

  • Where do they start? At the top-left corner of the map — the north-west most WAG. This gives every FLW a consistent, predictable starting point.
  • Where do they go next? Once a WAG is fully completed (every WA in it is done), the app looks at all remaining WAGs and picks the one nearest to where they just finished. No more being sent to a far corner of the territory and then back again.
  • The overall effect: the FLW sweeps across their territory like reading a page — start at the top corner, always move to the closest remaining WAG — which cuts down travel time and keeps their daily movement sensible.
  • What if map data is missing? The app quietly falls back to the old way of picking a WAG, so no one is ever stuck without an assignment.
The FLW does not choose WAGs themselves

The app decides automatically. They only confirm the switch when they finish a WAG.

Four WAGs assigned to one FLW. Distances are illustrative — the app scores the first WAG by wag_lat − wag_lon and the next by distance() from the current WAG's centroid.

How it works — technical explanation

Data prerequisites

  • Every work-area case carries: work_area_group (WAG name), hq_status_wa (visit status; empty = unvisited), and wag_centroid — the WAG's center point stored as "lat lon" (space-separated). All WAs of one WAG share the same centroid.
  • The FLW's active WAG is held in the user property current_work_area_group; the case list is filtered to it.

Core pattern. XPath cannot sort a nodeset by a computed value, so both parts use a hidden repeat-loop pattern: build a space-separated list of candidate WAG names, spawn one repeat iteration per candidate, compute a score/distance per iteration via hidden values, then select the row matching the min/max.

First WAG flow: no current_work_area_group → all unstarted WAGs are candidates → highest nw_score (= latitude − longitude) wins → saved to the user property.

Next WAG flow: switching is gated — the FLW can ask for the next WAG only when every WA in the current WAG is attended: it has visits (if the FLW could go in), or it is marked inaccessible (if they couldn't). A pending inaccessible request blocks the switch, as do unvisited and inaccessible-rejected WAs — the form shows the FLW exactly which WAs are blocking. Once eligible: candidates = unstarted WAGs excluding current → smallest wag_dist from the current WAG's centroid wins → current WAG's WA cases are closed → user property updated.

Fallback

Rows with blank/malformed centroids are excluded by the != '' predicates rather than erroring; the final assignment uses coalesce(geo_result, old_list_based_pick) so a blank geo-result silently reverts to legacy behavior.

Known limitation — WAG names with spaces

selected-at() / count-selected() split on spaces, so WAG names containing spaces (e.g. "Rainer Valley-1") fragment into invalid items and break both forms. Connect-generated names are space-free and safe; manually uploaded names must avoid spaces. Workaround for affected users: set current_work_area_group manually from HQ — do not use the Change WAG form, which shares the same defect.

FAQ

Section 4
Tech
Why are there two Work Area status properties (hq_status_wa vs wa_status)?

They track different lifecycles. hq_status_wa (HQ) records whether the FLW has worked in the WA at all — it flips to visited as soon as a household is registered. wa_status (Connect) records whether payable delivery happened — it only flips to VISITED on a child service delivery or a No U5 Children form. Example: household + child registered → HQ says visited, Connect still says NOT_VISITED; child delivered → both say visited.

Which case property stores the WAG identifier?

work_area_group on the work-area case (shown on Connect as "Work Area Group"). It is not wag_id.

How does the app decide which WAG comes next?

Geographically, via Auto-WAG Selection (from Master app v1949): the first WAG is the north-west most (highest latitude − longitude score over the WAG centroids), and each next WAG is the one whose wag_centroid is closest to the current WAG's centroid — computed with a hidden repeat-loop since XPath can't sort. No serial numbers are uploaded (wag_serial_number was rejected because the order can only be known after clustering and assignment). If centroid data is blank or malformed, the app falls back to the legacy list-based pick (first WAG with unvisited WAs).

How does auto-checkin work?

Each field form carries a GPS block plus a verification block. After the GPS reading meets the accuracy threshold, the form checks the coordinates against the boundaries of the WAs in the FLW's current WAG (centroid + inside_polygon() boundary logic) and attaches the submission to the WA the FLW is standing in — updating that WA's status automatically. There is no separate check-in form (that was the 2025 design).

What formats do centroid and bounding_box need?

centroid must be plain <lat> <long>, and bounding_box must be space-separated coordinate pairs consumable by inside_polygon() — e.g. 28.56735227 77.38577810 28.56735227 77.38680018 … closing back at the first pair. Raw exports arrive with SRID=4326;POINT / SRID=4326;POLYGON(( prefixes that must be stripped, or the app's location checks fail.

What is the expected default value of wa_status?

The Deliver app is designed to expect an empty value as the unvisited default. Uploads that set NOT_STARTED as the default break the workflow logic (the WAG-progression query and the No Children Found display condition both key off wa_status = '').

How do forms distinguish a rejected inaccessible request from a pending or approved one?

Only by reading both status properties. hq_status_wa stays inaccessible_requested forever once a request is filed — approval and rejection never change it. Connect's wa_status carries the outcome: REQUEST_FOR_INACCESSIBLE (pending), INACCESSIBLE (approved), NOT_VISITED (rejected). A rejected WA is workable, so the reference blocking pattern is:

not(
  current_hq_status_wa = 'inaccessible_requested'
  and (current_wa_status = 'INACCESSIBLE'
       or current_wa_status = 'REQUEST_FOR_INACCESSIBLE')
)

Any condition that blocks on hq_status_wa = 'inaccessible_requested' alone will wrongly block rejected WAs — this exact bug was fixed across the Household, No Children Found, and Inaccessible WA forms (August 2026). Related conventions: no-children detection uses the dedicated filled_no_children_found_wa = 'yes' work-area property, not the legacy check on wa_checkout_remark != '' (which over-matched); activity guards use the work-area counts delivered_visit_count and household_count as a status-independent backstop (written blank-safe: not(count > 0), so unset properties pass); conditionals in calculations use cond(); and label display conditions and their validation conditions stay exact mirrors so the UI and enforcement never disagree.

Why do WAG names with spaces break the app?

Auto-WAG Selection and the Change WAG form build space-separated candidate lists and read them with selected-at() / count-selected(), which split on spaces — a name like "Rainer Valley-1" fragments into invalid items. Connect-generated WAG names are space-free and safe; manually uploaded names must avoid spaces. Workaround for an affected user: set current_work_area_group manually from HQ (do not use the Change WAG form — it shares the defect).

How does the relearn lock/unlock mechanism work technically?

A task triggered on Connect sets a designated user property to 1. The Deliver app's form display conditions read it and hide all deliver-related forms, showing only the relearn module that matches the triggered task (deworming, MUAC, service delivery, etc.). The FLW must pass the module's quiz — randomised questions from that task's category, 100 marks to pass — and submit the Continue Service Delivery form, which submits the task question: the task is marked finished on Connect and all relearn properties reset, restoring the deliver forms. Nothing runs on CommCare HQ. Note that Connect rejects visits submitted while the task is pending.

What breaks if question_id or question_index has gaps?

The Learn randomiser generates IDs q1…qN up to maximum_number_of_question; a missing ID returns no rows and renders an empty question slot. The relearn randomiser does the same with question_index within a category. After any deletion, re-number to stay continuous, keep exactly one answer_status = 1 row per question, and update maximum_number_of_question.

What is Save to Case used for?

Save to Case questions manipulate cases without following the standard Case Management setup — create, update, close, and index from within the form builder. In CHC it's mainly used for conditional case updates (e.g. creating the child case from the registration repeat group and indexing it to the household).

Are there known GPS edge cases?

Yes — where two workers' WAs are contiguous, the combined allowance (25m minimum accuracy + 15m boundary leeway = up to 40m) can let one worker's submissions land in the neighbouring worker's WA. Also note the thresholds are uniform across forms (preferred <15m, minimum <25m) — relaxed from tighter values that proved hard to reach in urban testing — and the background auto-capture custom properties (cc-gps-auto-capture-accuracy: 15m, cc-gps-auto-capture-timeout: 10 min) keep a fresh reading available before a form opens.

Delivery
How do I add, edit, or remove assessment questions?

Edit the post_assess lookup table — remember each question spans multiple rows (one per option). Then run the checklist:

  • Exactly one row per question has answer_status = 1; question_text identical across a question's rows.
  • question_id continuous q1…qN (re-number after deletions).
  • question_index continuous 1…n within each category; each category keeps ≥ 5 questions.
  • Update maximum_number_of_question to the new bank size; confirm maximum_random_question_count_tobeshown ≤ bank size (Learn) and ≤ smallest category size (Relearn).
How do I trigger a relearn module for an FLW?

Entirely from Connect — nothing on CommCare HQ. From Connect audits, trigger the task (for one user or multiple users); it sets the relearn user property and the FLW's Deliver app locks to the matching relearn module on next sync. The FLW must complete the module, score 100 on its quiz, and submit the Continue Service Delivery form — that marks the task finished on Connect and unlocks the app automatically. Remember: while the task is pending, Connect rejects the FLW's visits. Task types must be configured on Connect before the opp launches.

What clustering settings should we use?

The delivery team's accepted proposal: max_number_of_wa_in_wag = 50 and max_count_of_building_in_wag = 200, whichever hits first. Watch for known algorithm issues: overlapping WAG boundaries, fragmented (non-contiguous) WAGs, high variance in WAs-per-WAG (1–107 observed), and "singleton" WAGs with a single WA — 54 of them appeared in one test run. Singletons and undersized WAGs are consolidated manually at clustering time; adjacent WAGs are preferably assigned to the same FLW.

What should I check before accepting a clustering output?
  • No WAG overlaps; closed polygons only; contiguous single-polygon WAGs.
  • No building-covered areas missed; nothing outside the opp area.
  • WA size intact (50×50m); unique colour coding per WAG.
  • Building count per WAG roughly uniform; WAs-per-WAG variance low.
Which verification flags should be configured on the opp?

Duplicates: yes — one rule, against form.check_child_duplicate_casedb (> 0 when a child with the same name + DOB already exists in the user's casedb), on the Service Delivery Unit only. It must be set up at opp-creation time or the rule won't fire. Suspicious DOB gap: no longer used — form.check_dob_gap_suspicious_duplicate is still calculated by the app but is not configured as a rule and has no effect. Working hours: 07:00 AM–06:00 PM; visits outside the window are flagged. Location distance: no — the app's GPS checks already cover it.

How should visit targets be planned?

Use saturation targets, not visit targets, with buffer visits and time built in. Denominator estimates (Grid3 / Geopode / Overture building data all have significant weaknesses) should be treated as ±40%. Contract a lower payable-visit maximum and be prepared for substantially more (e.g. present 150k payable visits, prepare for 250k), with reaction mechanisms when camping (oversaturation of one spot) is observed.

Can Work Areas be moved between FLWs?

Yes at assignment level, but with auto-checkin it carries extra work — reassignment requires updating the lookup/case data in CCHQ so the app's WAG resolution reflects the new owner. Reassigning adjacent WAGs to better performers is easier when FLWs were never shown the full list of WAGs they'd receive (which is the chosen approach).

LLO
What counts as a payable visit?

A child visit pays when every one of the four services (deworming, MUAC, ORS/Zinc, vaccine counseling) was either delivered or had a documented valid exemption — child too young, dose within the last 6 months, child unwell today, or MUAC consent refused. Any service skipped without a recorded reason makes the visit non-payable. The FLW sees this on the summary screen before leaving the household.

Are FLWs paid for Work Areas with no children?

Yes — the No Children Found WA deliver unit pays the equivalent of one child visit when an FLW properly reports a WA with no eligible U5 children (with reason and photo evidence). This recognizes effort in sparse areas, balanced against gaming risk: the form blocks submission if prior deliveries exist for that WA, and requires the FLW to confirm they covered the entire WA.

How do I review inaccessible Work Area requests?

On Connect, open Review Inaccessible WAs. You'll see workers with pending requests; expand a worker to list their requested WAs, click a WA for its details (reason, free-text explanation, mandatory photo, GPS, visit date), and approve or reject in the row — the review date auto-populates. Approve → the WA's Connect status becomes INACCESSIBLE and it counts as attended for WAG switching. Reject → only wa_status is sent back to HQ as NOT_VISITED (the recorded reason is kept), and the FLW is notified; the WA becomes workable again — when the FLW arrives, the household form shows them a rejection notice and allows registration. Either way, hq_status_wa on the phone stays inaccessible_requested — the black map marker does not change. The review outcome syncs from Connect to HQ in realtime. FLWs cannot move to their next WAG until every WA in the group is resolved, so review requests promptly.

What do the map colours mean?

No fill, no point — not started. Light yellow fill (#FFF9C4) / orange point (#F9A825) — visited (a household was registered or the No Children WA form was filled). Black point (#000000), no fill — inaccessible requested; the marker stays black even after you approve or reject the request on Connect, because the HQ-side status never changes from inaccessible_requested.

How is duplicate or fraudulent data caught?

Several layers: GPS verification on every form (registration inside the WA; delivery within 50m of the household; No-Children only inside the WA), mandatory photo evidence (MUAC reading and ORS handover; building photo for no-children WAs), duplicate detection (in-app blocking on household and child names; HSD-form duplicate flags rejected via Connect verification rules), and relearn tasks. NMs supplement this with dashboard monitoring and spot checks; while a relearn task is pending, Connect rejects the FLW's visits.

When does an FLW get flagged for relearning?

During review of submissions in Connect audits — inconsistent data, low submission volume, or other quality metrics. A task is triggered from Connect (for one FLW or several at once); the FLW's app locks to the matching relearn module (deworming, MUAC, service delivery, etc.) until they pass its quiz with 100 marks. While the task is pending, Connect rejects that FLW's visits, so the intervention directly pauses payable work.

FLW
Why does the app keep asking for my location?

Your location proves you are inside your assigned Work Area — it's how every service is verified for payment. If you see Improve Location Accuracy, press Record Location and wait up to a minute, preferably outdoors with a clear sky view. Once accuracy is good enough the app tells you which Work Area you are in and lets you continue.

I have claimed the Deliver job — can I start straight away?

Yes. There is no waiting period and no approval step in the app: sync the app, submit the Welcome to CHC form, and your first Work Area Group is set automatically. If no Work Area Group appears, your Work Areas have not been assigned yet — tell your Network Manager. (Older builds held FLWs on a wait screen until the team enabled them; that gate was removed.)

How does the Learn assessment quiz work? What score do I need?

The quiz unlocks automatically once you complete every learn module. It has 20 questions and you need 80 marks — 16 of 20 correct — to pass, with unlimited attempts. When you pass, you get a push notification on Connect that Learn is finished; go to the view job status page and claim the Deliver job. (A relearn quiz, if a task is assigned to you later, requires a full 100 to pass.)

Where do I get help using the app?

The OCS support bot is available in the Connect Messaging module — it's triggered once you fill the Learn app. It helps with app navigation and answers common questions. Your Network Manager remains the escalation point for anything the bot can't resolve.

When can I use the "No Children Found" form?

Only when you have visited the entire Work Area and found no eligible under-5 children — and only if you haven't submitted anything else for that WA yet. You'll confirm your intent, pick the reason (no buildings, access denied, no one home, etc.), and take a photo of a building inside the WA as evidence. If children were actually found, the form blocks you and points you to the Health Service Delivery form instead. A properly reported no-children WA pays the same as one child visit.

Can I come back to a household to serve a child who was away?

Yes — children registered but not yet served stay in your case list, and you can return to complete their service delivery. Exception: a child marked as unwell in the service delivery form cannot be revisited for that delivery — you are still paid for that first visit.

What do ✅, ⚠️ and ❌ mean on the summary screen?

✅ Delivered — you provided the service. ⚠️ Ineligible / No Consent — the service couldn't be given for a valid recorded reason (child too young, recently dosed, sick today, consent refused); this still counts toward payment. ❌ Not Delivered — the service was skipped without a valid reason, and the visit will not be payable. Check the summary before leaving the household — a ❌ tells you exactly which service to fix.

Why was my visit not counted for payment?

One or more of the four services shows ❌ — most commonly a missing photo (MUAC reading or ORS handover), an unconfirmed handover, or a skipped service with no reason recorded. Every service needs either a delivery or a valid documented reason. Also make sure you were within 50m of where the household was registered when delivering.

How do I take a MUAC photo that gets approved?

Take the photo after reading the measurement, while the tape is still on the arm. Child's arm by the side of the body, elbow bent about 90°. Hold the phone straight-on, level with the tape, far enough back that you can see: the arm centred, elbow and forearm at the bottom, side of the torso behind, and the tape around the middle of the left upper arm with bare skin above and below it.

Rejected if: the tape is on any body part other than the mid upper arm; the photo is so zoomed-in the reading window, colour zone or surrounding skin can't be seen; the tape is skewed in the reading window. Avoid: pulling the tape too tight, glare on the reading window, fingers blocking the window, measuring near the elbow/shoulder, extended elbow.

How do I take an ORS photo that gets approved?

Take the photo after handing the packet to the caregiver, while they are holding it in front of their body. Hold the phone straight-on and level with the packet; all four edges of the packet visible, front label facing the camera, caregiver's clothes visible behind.

Rejected if: the packet is lying on a surface instead of held; the back label is captured; another object (e.g. zinc tablets) obscures the packet. Avoid: glare on the label.

How do I get my next Work Area Group?

Finish every Work Area in your current WAG — each WA must be delivered, reported as no-children, or approved as inaccessible. Then use Request New WAG; the app assigns your next group automatically. If a WA is stuck on a pending inaccessible request, your Network Manager needs to approve or reject it first.

What if I can't reach a Work Area at all?

Submit the Inaccessible WA request with the reason (flood, conflict, road damage, etc.), details, and a mandatory photo. This is irreversible — once submitted it cannot be withdrawn, and you can't submit it for a WA where you've already registered a household. Your Network Manager reviews it on Connect: approved means the WA counts as attended and you won't need to return (the marker on your map stays black); rejected means it returns to your list to visit — when you arrive there, the household form shows a notice that your request was rejected and lets you register households normally. While a request is pending you cannot submit another one for that WA — and a pending request also blocks you from switching to your next WAG.

What consent do I need before registering a family?

Read the caregiver consent statement and record their answer before proceeding. It covers participation, recording the child's basic details (name, age, sex, illness history), MUAC measurement, and giving ORS/Zinc and deworming medicine. The caregiver can refuse — and can revoke consent at any point during the intervention. If MUAC consent specifically is refused, record it; the visit can still be payable with that exemption documented.
